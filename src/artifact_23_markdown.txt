1/7 🧵 Inside the Block: How @Solana Validators ACTUALLY Order Transactions.

Think Solana has a public mempool like Ethereum? Think again. There is no global queue.

Here is the technical reality of TPU, QUIC, SWQoS, write-locks, and the Agave scheduler 👇

2/7 ⚙️ Step 1: TPU Ingress & Stake-Weighted QoS (SWQoS)
Your transaction doesn't broadcast to the whole network. It streams over QUIC directly to the current & upcoming slot leaders.
Staked nodes get dedicated pipe allocations. Unstaked RPCs fight for the remaining ~20% buffer. Under load? Drops occur at the socket layer.

3/7 🔒 Step 2: The Parallel Execution Illusion & Write-Locks
Sealevel executes txs in parallel across threads—UNLESS they write to the same account.
Two txs updating the same AMM pool CANNOT run concurrently.
Max Block Limit: 48M Compute Units.
Max Single Account Limit: 12M CUs (preventing single-pool monopolies).

4/7 💰 Step 3: Priority Fee Sorting (Micro-Lamports per CU)
Solana sorts non-conflicting batches greedily:
Priority Score = Prioritization Fee / Compute Budget Requested.
Overpaying priority fees on an account that has already hit its 12M CU limit will still get your tx dropped!

5/7 ⚡ Step 4: Out-of-Band Jito-Solana Auctions
Over 75% of validators run Jito.
Searchers send atomic bundles off-chain with direct SOL tips. Jito's block engine simulates bundles and injects them straight into BankingStage, bypassing standard TPU queues.
